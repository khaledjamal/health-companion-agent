Heart Rate Variability (HRV) and Recovery

HRV is one of the most reliable indicators of recovery status and autonomic nervous system balance. Understanding and optimizing HRV is crucial for health and performance.

What is HRV:
- HRV measures variation in time between heartbeats
- Higher HRV generally indicates better recovery and stress resilience
- HRV is controlled by the autonomic nervous system
- Normal range varies by individual (typically 20-100ms for RMSSD)

HRV as a Recovery Marker:
- High HRV: body is recovered and ready for stress/training
- Low HRV: body needs more recovery time
- Declining HRV trend: accumulating stress or overtraining
- HRV drops during illness, overtraining, or high stress

Factors that Improve HRV:
- Quality sleep (7-9 hours)
- Regular moderate exercise
- Stress management (meditation, breathwork)
- Proper hydration
- Balanced nutrition
- Recovery activities (massage, sauna, cold therapy)
- Consistent sleep schedule

Factors that Decrease HRV:
- Poor sleep quality or insufficient sleep
- Overtraining without adequate recovery
- Excessive stress (physical or psychological)
- Dehydration
- Alcohol consumption
- Illness or infection
- Poor diet quality

Using HRV for Decision Making:
- Check HRV first thing in the morning for most accurate reading
- If HRV is significantly below baseline (>10%): prioritize recovery
- If HRV is normal or elevated: okay to proceed with normal activities
- Track trends over weeks, not just daily values
- Combine HRV with subjective feelings and other metrics

HRV and Training:
- Low HRV suggests nervous system fatigue - reduce training intensity
- Consistently high HRV indicates good adaptation to training
- Use HRV to guide recovery day decisions
- Plan rest days when HRV is declining over multiple days

Recovery Strategies for Low HRV:
- Prioritize 8+ hours of quality sleep
- Reduce training intensity or take rest day
- Practice stress-reduction techniques
- Increase hydration
- Gentle movement (walking, yoga, stretching)
- Avoid alcohol and excessive caffeine
- Consider recovery modalities (massage, sauna)

HRV and Stress:
- Psychological stress impacts HRV similar to physical stress
- Chronic stress leads to persistently low HRV
- Meditation and breathwork can acutely increase HRV
- Managing life stress is crucial for HRV optimization

Measurement Best Practices:
- Measure at same time daily (upon waking is ideal)
- Measure in same position (lying or sitting)
- Take 1-3 minute reading for accuracy
- Track trends over 7-30 days, not single readings
- Use consistent measurement device/method

When HRV is Persistently Low:
- Review sleep quality and duration
- Assess training volume and intensity
- Evaluate stress levels (work, relationships, life)
- Check for signs of overtraining or illness
- Consider 1-2 week deload period
- Consult healthcare provider if concerns persist
