Sleep Optimization Guidelines

Quality sleep is fundamental to health, recovery, and performance. Here are evidence-based strategies for optimizing sleep:

Sleep Duration:
- Adults need 7-9 hours of sleep per night for optimal health
- Consistency is key: maintain regular sleep and wake times, even on weekends
- Sleep debt accumulates and cannot be fully recovered with weekend catch-up sleep

Sleep Environment:
- Keep bedroom temperature between 60-67°F (15-19°C)
- Minimize light exposure: use blackout curtains or eye masks
- Reduce noise: use earplugs or white noise machines if needed
- Ensure comfortable mattress and pillow support

Pre-Sleep Routine:
- Avoid caffeine 8-10 hours before bedtime
- Limit alcohol consumption (disrupts REM sleep)
- Avoid large meals 2-3 hours before sleep
- Reduce blue light exposure 2 hours before bed
- Establish a relaxing wind-down routine (reading, meditation, gentle stretching)

Sleep and HRV:
- Heart Rate Variability (HRV) is strongly correlated with sleep quality
- Deep sleep stages are critical for HRV recovery
- Low HRV often indicates insufficient recovery and sleep issues
- Prioritize sleep when HRV is consistently below your baseline

Common Sleep Disruptors:
- Electronic devices and screen time before bed
- Irregular sleep schedule
- Stress and anxiety
- Lack of physical activity during the day
- Poor sleep environment (light, noise, temperature)

Sleep Tracking:
- Monitor sleep patterns to identify trends
- Pay attention to sleep efficiency (time asleep / time in bed)
- Track how different behaviors affect sleep quality
- Use data to make informed adjustments to sleep hygiene

Recovery Indicators:
- Improved morning HRV indicates good sleep quality
- Lower resting heart rate suggests adequate recovery
- Consistent energy levels throughout the day
- Better mood and cognitive performance

When to Seek Help:
- Chronic difficulty falling or staying asleep (>3 weeks)
- Excessive daytime sleepiness despite adequate sleep duration
- Loud snoring or breathing pauses during sleep
- Persistent low HRV despite good sleep hygiene
