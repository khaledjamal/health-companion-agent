Nutrition Guidelines for Health and Performance

Proper nutrition supports recovery, energy levels, cognitive function, and overall health. These guidelines provide evidence-based nutritional strategies.

Macronutrient Balance:
- Protein: 0.8-1.2g per kg body weight for general health, up to 2g/kg for athletes
- Carbohydrates: Primary energy source, 3-7g/kg based on activity level
- Fats: Essential for hormone production, 0.8-1.2g/kg body weight
- Adjust ratios based on individual response and goals

Hydration:
- Drink 30-40ml per kg body weight daily
- Increase intake during exercise and hot weather
- Monitor urine color: pale yellow indicates good hydration
- Dehydration negatively impacts HRV and recovery
- Electrolyte balance important for athletes and active individuals

Meal Timing:
- Eat within 1-2 hours of waking to support metabolism
- Space meals 3-4 hours apart for stable energy
- Post-exercise nutrition within 30-90 minutes aids recovery
- Avoid large meals 2-3 hours before bedtime
- Consider light protein before bed for overnight recovery

Food Quality:
- Prioritize whole, minimally processed foods
- Include variety of colorful vegetables and fruits
- Choose lean protein sources
- Include healthy fats (omega-3s, nuts, avocado, olive oil)
- Limit added sugars and ultra-processed foods

Nutrition for Recovery:
- Adequate protein supports muscle repair and recovery
- Antioxidant-rich foods reduce inflammation
- Omega-3 fatty acids support recovery and reduce soreness
- Proper carbohydrate intake replenishes glycogen stores
- Micronutrients (vitamins, minerals) crucial for recovery processes

Performance Nutrition:
- Pre-exercise: easily digestible carbs 1-3 hours before
- During exercise: carbs for sessions >90 minutes
- Post-exercise: protein + carbs within recovery window
- Maintain consistent energy intake to support training
- Periodize nutrition based on training demands

Sleep and Nutrition Connection:
- Avoid caffeine 8-10 hours before bedtime
- Limit alcohol (disrupts sleep architecture)
- Avoid heavy meals close to bedtime
- Some benefit from small protein snack before bed
- Magnesium-rich foods may support sleep quality

Supplements to Consider:
- Vitamin D if deficient (common in many populations)
- Omega-3 fatty acids if not consuming fatty fish regularly
- Magnesium for sleep and recovery
- Probiotics for gut health
- Creatine for performance (if training intensely)
- Always prioritize whole foods over supplements

Stress and Nutrition:
- Stress increases nutrient needs
- Adequate nutrition supports stress resilience
- Blood sugar stability affects stress response
- Avoid using food as primary stress management tool
- Balance macros to prevent energy crashes

Common Nutritional Mistakes:
- Insufficient protein intake
- Chronic under-eating relative to activity level
- Excessive caffeine consumption
- Poor meal timing around sleep
- Inadequate hydration
- Over-reliance on processed foods
- Ignoring individual food sensitivities

Individualization:
- Optimal nutrition varies by individual
- Track how different foods affect energy and recovery
- Consider food sensitivities and allergies
- Adjust based on training phase and goals
- Work with nutrition professional for personalized guidance
- Use data (HRV, energy, performance) to guide choices

Practical Tips:
- Meal prep to ensure healthy options available
- Keep healthy snacks accessible
- Plan meals around training schedule
- Stay consistent with eating patterns
- Don't restrict too severely - sustainability matters
- Allow flexibility for social situations
- Focus on progress, not perfection
